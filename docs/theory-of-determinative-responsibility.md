# Theory of Determinative Responsibility

> **Knowledge and evidence boundary:** This document is the meta-model and minimum-language layer of the knowledge system. It defines epistemic boundaries, the minimum determinable structure, the analytical main chain by which finite systems intervene in reality, the analysis layers, and the fact–norm boundary. It does not claim empirical scientific laws and does not replace domain models. Concrete objects, mechanisms, facts, causality, determinations of subjectivity, intervention effects, and professional standards are owned by domain models, situational evidence, and professional standards.
>
> **Core discipline:** Remain silent where determination cannot legitimately continue; within determinable reality, form fallible structures while preserving reality's power to revise distinctions, factors, organization, orientation, judgment, and action.

## Definition Dependencies

The V1 public interface of this document includes `E₀`, `E₁`, difference, retention, distinction, factor, organizational structure, orientation, judgment, action, correction, open / closure, the three analysis layers, and the minimum normative boundary. Candidate invariants, quantitative change, interruption of quantitative change, and qualitative change may still be rigorously defined here, but as document-local working vocabulary; their appearance in the prose does not expand the platform-level canonical surface.

[Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](03-theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md) specializes the upstream concepts at the mechanism level; [Responsibility Structure Candidate Catalog](06-responsibility-structure-candidate-catalog.md) preserves transferable candidates; domain models own concrete mechanisms and evidence; [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md) owns proceduralization. When machine recording is required, responsibility is handed to the [Responsibility Record Plane](09-responsibility-record-plane.md), but this document does not import its epistemic states, lifecycles, or other record tokens. Downstream documents must not silently change the public definitions here.

## I. Epistemic Boundary and Minimum Determinable Structure

### 1. `E₀`: boundary marker for stopping further determination

`E₀` marks **where no differentiating determination can yet legitimately be applied further**.

`E₀` is not an object, a state, primordial being, nothingness, chaos, pure being, a temporal origin, a stage of change, a function input, a causal antecedent, or a hidden entity. Describing it as “some kind of thing” has already imposed a distinction again.

It therefore states only an epistemic entitlement:

$$
\text{further legitimate determination is unavailable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{reality has no corresponding structure}
$$

and:

$$
\text{a stable structure has currently been obtained}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{the ultimate structure of reality has been obtained}
$$

Silence about `E₀` is not a statement about its properties. It is the discipline by which a finite system stops unauthorized determination. A theory may add conclusions that cannot legitimately be drawn; it may not use this to add predicates to `E₀`.

### 2. `E₁`: existence

`E₁` marks **the range within which differentiating determination can legitimately unfold**. Except where the epistemic boundary `E₀` is explicitly discussed, “existence” in this meta-model always refers to `E₁`.

`E₀` and `E₁` form an asymmetric contrast in the entitlement to determine: `E₀` is not determined as either existent or nonexistent; `E₁` is the side on which existence and further differentiating determination may legitimately be used. They are not two known states and do not presuppose temporal order, causal relation, generation, or manifestation.

Therefore:

$$
E_0\neq\text{nonexistence}
$$

$$
E_0\rightarrow E_1
\quad\text{is not a claim of this document}
$$

At the same time:

$$
E_1
\quad\text{does not mean reality has been completely obtained by a finite system}
$$

Continuity, interruption, cycles, qualitative change, and similar determinations all belong on the `E₁` side. They are not used to determine the relation between `E₀` and `E₁`.

### 3. Difference, retention, and candidate invariants

Any structure in `E₁` that can be further determined, compared, identified, or discussed over time requires at minimum **difference** and **retention**.

| Concept | Theoretical status | Minimum meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Difference** | Minimum condition of determinability | The current distinction contains items, states, or relations that cannot be treated as completely identical |
| **Retention** | Minimum condition of determinability | Some rule of distinction, identity, relation, constraint, or continuity continues to hold under specified transformations, scales, and conditions |
| **Candidate invariant** | A concrete, fallible account of retention | A revisable model of “what remains” after the object, allowed transformations, scale, time, conditions, and evidence are specified |

With difference but no retention, earlier and later records cannot be compared as the same object, rule, or relation. With retention but no difference, there are no states, relations, or changes to distinguish.

Difference and retention are minimum conditions for forming further determinable structure in `E₁`; this does not mean that `E₁` is composed of difference and retention, that they exhaust `E₁`, or that they may be used to impose determinations on `E₀`.

**Continuity is not the absence of difference; it is the persistence of enough retention to establish correspondence before and after differences occur.** Apparent discontinuity therefore cannot by itself distinguish interruption of a process from change in the retained structure itself.

Concrete claims about differences and candidate invariants depend on object boundaries, standards of identity or equivalence, observation conditions, scale, and evidence. The structure currently obtained by a finite system therefore cannot be identified with reality in `E₁` itself.

Cross-context identity or comparability must likewise be supported by an explicit standard of retention. The same name, field, dimension, role, or formal position is not enough to prove that what has been obtained in two contexts is the same object, the same factor, or a directly comparable state. One must state what is allowed to change, what is required to remain, and within what scope the correspondence holds.

### 4. Distinction

**Distinction** is the process or functional relation by which a finite system, under its current boundary, channels, scale, and purpose, turns real inputs available to current processing into usable differences that can be separated or merged, compared, and passed into subsequent processing.

Distinction does not fully define the mechanism of connection to and acquisition from reality. How perception, measurement, sampling, logs, retrieval, interviews, experimental apparatus, or other connections obtain inputs is outside the minimum meta-model definition. When the acquisition mechanism itself becomes an important object of judgment, [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](03-theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md) explicitly specializes it as material acquisition $\mathcal Q$.

A viable distinction requires both a differentiated result and enough retention to recognize, compare, or invoke that difference repeatedly. Distinction does not guarantee truth: it may omit key items, merge them incorrectly, split them excessively, or form a stable classification at the wrong scale that has no action value.

## II. Analytical Main Chain of Finite-System Intervention in Reality

### 1. Main chain

A finite system's intervention in reality can be organized by the following analytical main chain:

$$
R_t
\xrightarrow{D_t}
F_t
\xrightarrow{\mathcal O_t}
S_t
\xrightarrow{J_t}
A_t
\rightarrow
R_{t+1}
$$

where:

- $R_t$: the current real situation in `E₁` and the part of it capable of entering relations with the system;
- $D_t$: the current distinction;
- $F_t$: factors retained under the current boundary, scale, and purpose;
- $\mathcal O_t$: the organizing process by which factors form classifications, relations, sequences, constraints, orientations, or other structures;
- $S_t$: the organizational structure currently formed and available for invocation;
- $J_t$: the integrated selection of “what to provisionally adopt next” under evidence, unknowns, orientation, authority, value, risk, and possible consequences;
- $A_t$: intervention implemented under real-world conditions and authority;
- $R_{t+1}$: the new reality after the combined effects of action, environmental change, and other subjects.

This chain is an **unfolding order of analytical responsibility** and lies entirely on the `E₁` side. It is not a genesis of reality and does not describe generation, transformation, manifestation, or temporal order between `E₀` and `E₁`.

In the main chain,

$$
R_t\xrightarrow{D_t}F_t
$$

represents the analytical responsibility by which constraints from reality enter the current distinction and form factors. It does not imply that a finite system can acquire reality directly without mediation, nor does it require every cycle to model connection to reality independently as $\mathcal Q$.

The current distinction is usually already shaped by existing structure, orientation, history, and conditions:

$$
D_t
D_t =
D(R_t\mid S_t^{-},G_t^{-},\mathcal H_t,C_t,\ldots)
$$

The organizing process is likewise not neutral:

$$
\mathcal O_t
\mathcal O_t =
\mathcal O(F_t\mid S_t^{-},G_t^{-},\mathcal H_t,C_t,\ldots)
$$

Existing structures and orientations affect which factors the system obtains, which relations seem worth searching for, which combinations currently matter, and which organizational forms more readily become candidates. The main chain therefore must not be interpreted as first obtaining a structure-independent, theoretically complete set of factors and only then adding relations to it.

New reality also feeds back into the next round of analysis:

$$
R_{t+1}
\rightarrow
\{D,F,S,G,J,A\}_{t+1}
$$

Within a specified object, scale, and scope, if the principal retained structure still holds, subsequent change is **quantitative change**. Repeated quantitative change under the same principal retained structure may form a cycle; a cycle does not mean returning to the original point.

If a quantitative process stops, pauses, or loses the conditions required to continue, but this is insufficient to show that the principal retained structure has changed, this is an **interruption of quantitative change**.

If the principal retained structure that previously served as the background of quantitative change itself changes, such that subsequent change can no longer be adequately determined only under the previous structure, this is **qualitative change**.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{interruption of quantitative change}
\neq
\text{qualitative change}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{apparent discontinuity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{qualitative change}
}
$$

Quantitative and qualitative change both depend on the object, scale, scope, and standard of retention. Continuous quantitative change at one scale may correspond to qualitative change at another. Concrete thresholds, critical conditions, and mechanisms are owned by domain models and evidence.

### 2. Local determinations and joint structure

The fact that local distinctions, factors, or structures can each be determined does not automatically imply a global joint structure that simultaneously preserves all local determinations. This theory adopts the following epistemic discipline:

$$
\boxed{
\text{separately determinable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{jointly determinable}
}
$$

Moving from local determinations to a joint representation requires separately obtaining adequate compatibility conditions, cross-context retention conditions, and domain constraints. The restriction does not say that a global structure does not exist; it says that **globalization is not free**.

Even if multiple local determinations can be jointly retained, it still does not follow that the local representations are sufficient to determine the joint organizational information:

$$
\boxed{
\text{jointly determinable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{local representations are sufficient to determine the joint structure}
}
$$

One must therefore distinguish whether each local determination is clear, whether the determinations are compatible, and whether they are sufficient to determine the joint structure presently under discussion. Concrete judgment of these questions belongs to [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](03-theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md) and domain models.

### 3. Factors

A **factor** is an item retained under an explicit system boundary, scale, problem, and mode of distinction so that it can enter later organization, comparison, judgment, or action. Objects, states, variables, roles, conditions, events, and records may all become factors.

Factors are not an ultimate inventory of the constituents of reality. Changing the distinction may change the factor set and thereby change causal, value, and action judgments. Current object types, variable lists, and state spaces must therefore always preserve the possibility of incompleteness.

### 4. Organizational structure and orientation

An **organizational structure** is a retainable structure among factors, including classification, identity, configuration, order, dependency, causality, feedback, constraints, value, orientation, roles, authority, relations, games, and other forms. See the [Responsibility Structure Candidate Catalog](06-responsibility-structure-candidate-catalog.md) for concrete candidates.

**Orientation** is a structure within organizational structure that gives possible states, actions, or retained conditions different salience, priority, approach/avoid directions, or completion criteria. Goals, values, and functional references may all form orientations, but their sources and status must be distinguished.

Structurally:

$$
G\subseteq S
$$

$G$ is still kept explicit in analysis because orientation raises special questions of source, endorsement, modification, conflict, authorization, and legitimacy, and directly participates in active distinction and judgment:

$$
G,S\rightarrow D,
\qquad
F,S,G\rightarrow J
$$

When no explicit goal representation exists, one may describe a functional persistence reference, stable tendency, or externally assigned task, but an observer's interpretation must not be written directly as the system's own endorsed purpose.

### 5. Judgment

**Judgment** is not synonymous with logical derivation, probabilistic computation, value ranking, decision, or action. It is an integrated selection, on top of current factors and organizational structure and under evidence, unknowns, orientation, value, authority, risk, and possible consequences, of “what to provisionally adopt next.”

Judgment may concern propositions, object partitions, candidate structures, orientations, action authorization, and whether a problem should remain open or is adequate for provisional closure. Causal structure matters for action judgment but cannot replace independent judgment about value, authority, responsibility, and non-compensable boundaries.

This can be compressed as:

$$
\boxed{\text{distinction points inward; judgment points outward}}
$$

Distinction determines which real differences enter the current processable structure. Judgment determines which structures, interpretations, orientations, or actions provisionally acquire downstream real-world effect.

### 6. Action, feedback, regulation, and correction

**Action** is intervention implemented under real-world conditions and authority. Technical executability does not imply that decision authority, authorization, or legitimacy has been obtained.

**Feedback** means that results re-enter a later process; **regulation** means that feedback actually changes the process; **correction** requires the system, under its current standards, functional references, purposes, or judgment procedure, to modify distinctions, structures, judgments, actions, or conditions. The three must not be substituted for one another.

Correction has no preset depth. A local anomaly does not automatically require global reconstruction, while persistent structural mismatch cannot indefinitely be downgraded into a parameter, execution, or measurement problem. Correction within a structure usually remains quantitative change; when the principal retained structure itself enters revision, the change may become qualitative. See [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](03-theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md) for the intelligence mechanism of revision depth and [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md) for proceduralization.

The epistemic discipline of this theory is: **form structures boldly, believe structures cautiously; allow reality to veto current distinctions, organization, and judgment.**

## III. Higher-Order Working Structure: Open and Closure

“Open / closure” is a higher-order working distinction, not a minimum primitive, and it does not claim that all epistemic or real change obeys a binary division.

An **open task** allows the mode of distinction, factor set, principal organizational structure, orientation, or problem boundary to continue changing. It is suitable for finding omissions, redrawing boundaries, generating candidates, and searching for counterexamples. Openness also permits reviewing whether the original problem wrongly assumed that several local determinations could enter the same joint structure. Where necessary, one may revise which determinations can be combined, how they may be combined, and which must remain conditional or separate.

A **closed task** temporarily fixes the principal $D/F/S/G$, constraints, and acceptance conditions within an explicit scope, and then performs formalization, calculation, implementation, testing, and detailed control:

$$
(D,F,S,G)
(D,F,S,G) =
\text{temporarily fixed within scope}
$$

Quantitative change usually occurs within the principal structure that is currently fixed; qualitative change means that at least part of the principal retained structure needs to be reopened. An interruption of quantitative change is not by itself a reason to reopen the structure.

**The purpose of closure must be distinguished.** A problem may have reached epistemic closure sufficient for continued comparison, derivation, computation, modeling, or current explanation, yet still be insufficient for real-world intervention. In addition to the current epistemic structure, real action must satisfy adequate conditions of purpose endorsement, decision authority, third-party boundaries, risk, resources, recovery, stopping, and responsibility. Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{sufficient to continue solving}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{sufficient to authorize action}
}
$$

These correspond to provisional closures for different purposes. Concrete action conditions are proceduralized by [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md).

When a compressed notation is needed, $C_t^E$ and $C_t^A$ may denote closure configurations for epistemic and action purposes respectively. They are working notation only; they do not denote fixed field sets and do not require a strict set-inclusion relation. Thus one may write:

$$
C_t^E\rightsquigarrow C_t^A
$$

rather than formally writing:

$$
C_t^A\supset C_t^E
$$

because the latter is too strong. In particular, “epistemic closure” must not be understood as a purely factual layer: $C^E$ may still include the current $G$, problem scope, loss structure, and so on; it simply has not yet acquired qualification for real-world intervention.

A mature system needs to be able to remain open, close proportionately, and reopen. Critical counterexamples, accumulated anomalies, proliferating exceptions, scale conflicts, changes in environment or goals, anomalous success, unexpected regularity, persistent residuals, and major internal incoherence may all trigger reopening.

Provisional closure should state its applicability boundary and reopening conditions as far as possible. Failure to fully state the boundary does not automatically invalidate a structure, but it should reduce confidence in cross-context migration and long-term persistence.

## IV. Three Analysis Layers

### 1. Reality-change layer

Studies objects, states, boundaries, relations, and changes in `E₁` that can be further determined by the current analysis, together with which structures remain under specified transformations, scales, and conditions.

Real change may occur entirely without subject intervention. Quantitative change, interruption of quantitative change, and qualitative change should be distinguished; concrete mechanisms and candidate invariants belong to domain models and evidence.

### 2. Finite-system intervention layer

Studies how a finite system, between existing structure and real-world input, forms distinctions, factors, and organization; how it judges and acts; and how results correct it. Whether the system is a subject, whether it endorses its orientations, and whether it bears responsibility require separate judgment.

### 3. Shared-affairs layer

Studies, among multiple subjects and systems that carry their tasks or affect their situations, how the capacities to distinguish, include factors, define structures, form orientations, judge, authorize, act, verify, correct, and stop are distributed, and how benefits, costs, losses, and responsibilities for repair are borne.

The three layers may enter one another but must not shortcut one another: mechanisms of reality, capabilities of finite systems, and power and legitimacy in shared affairs have different criteria.

## V. Normative Boundary

Factual structures, differences, causality, predictions, and real-world outcomes cannot by themselves derive what ought to be pursued. At minimum:

$$
\text{fact}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{purpose}
$$

$$
\text{capability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{authorization}
$$

$$
\text{causal effectiveness}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{value legitimacy}
$$

$$
\text{a goal is endorsed}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{it legitimately binds others}
$$

$$
\text{currently stable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{ought to continue existing}
$$

Purposes may be endorsed by subjects, jointly negotiated, institutionally undertaken, or externally assigned. These sources still do not automatically prove factual correctness, action effectiveness, or the legitimacy of an arrangement. Value ranking, authorization, rights, responsibility, and legitimacy each have independent criteria.

Normative orientations currently endorsed by a particular subject or platform are not stable definitions of this meta-model. They should be recorded as Goals / Policies / commitments with source, owner, scope, review conditions, and exit conditions, and should undergo independent normative argument and testing against real-world outcomes.

The permanent responsibility of this document is therefore to preserve the boundary showing which factual, capability, or causal conclusions cannot shortcut into normative conclusions—not to encode the variable commitments of “what should currently be pursued.”

When judgment acquires real-world effect, distinguish who proposed the structure, who endorsed the orientation, who has decision authority, who executes, who bears consequences, who is affected, and who can demand stopping, repair, or review. Capability, predictive accuracy, and degree of automation do not confer these positions by themselves.

- Domain models and situational facts: own concrete objects, mechanisms, causality, thresholds, and professional evidence.

- [Responsibility Record Plane](09-responsibility-record-plane.md): represents evidence, assertions, goals, decisions, actions, outcomes, and revisions when machine recording is required; its schema is not a definitional dependency of this document.

When modifying this document, give priority to the following tests: the proposition should still hold after concrete domain mechanisms are removed; a new concept must genuinely be inexpressible in the existing meta-model language; a new structure must state its epistemic status; and real-world counterexamples must be able to trigger narrowing, revision, or deletion rather than merely generating additional exceptions that make the theory unfalsifiable.

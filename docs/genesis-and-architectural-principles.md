# Genesis and Architectural Principles of Responsibility Topology

> **Document status:** This document only preserves why the framework formed its current responsibility structure, which shortcuts were rejected, and why some questions are intentionally left open. It does not own canonical definitions. Current owners, invocation relations, and governance rules are determined by [Responsibility Topology Overview](responsibility-topology-overview.md) and the `semantic_contract` of each canonical document.
>
> **Core question:** By what entitlement may a finite system situated inside reality—limited by time, channels, scale, history, and its own structure—extend a currently obtained determination into a stronger epistemic claim, value judgment, intersubjective constraint, or real-world action?

## I. Point of genesis: finitude is not pessimism

The framework's earliest question was not governance, correction, or responsibility, but epistemic entitlement:

> By what right can a finite knower inside the universe claim to have obtained the whole truth of the universe?

"Finite" first means a limitation of position, not merely a shortage of capability. A finite system is inside reality, participates in causal relations, and can obtain reality only through finite channels, scales, histories, and organizational structures. No "God's-eye view outside the universe" is naturally granted to it.

The resulting conclusion is not "knowledge is forever impossible," but:

> **A currently obtained determination does not receive unconditional entitlement to keep expanding.**

This became the common starting point from which the framework later differentiated.

---

## II. Retreat from ontological assertion to the boundary of determination

At an early stage it is tempting to treat "difference," "retention," and "distinction" as the world's lowest ontological primitives, for example:

> At the bottom of the world is distinction.

After further questioning, the more stable position became:

> Any real structure that a finite system can continue to determine, identify, compare, and discuss requires at least proportionate difference and retention. Distinction is a functional position by which a finite system forms processable differences, not a declaration about the ultimate constitution of reality.

This shift changed the type of the whole framework. It stopped trying to derive all reality from a single ontological principle and instead began to specify **how far a finite system is entitled to speak**.

`E₀` is the purest expression of this discipline: when differentiating determination can no longer be legitimately continued, determination stops. Stopping is not a statement about "what is there," nor does it imply that corresponding structure does not exist in reality.

The central meaning of `E₀`, therefore, is not to provide an answer but to preserve **theoretical stopping capacity**.

---

## III. General genesis chain

The generative logic of the framework can be compressed as:

$$
\boxed{
\text{finitude}
\rightarrow
\text{boundary of determination}
\rightarrow
\text{entitlement to determine}
\rightarrow
\text{differentiation of responsibility}
\rightarrow
\text{handoff / provisional closure / reopen / stop}
}
$$

Here, "entitlement to determine" is not a permanent property possessed by a system. It is a fallible entitlement formed relative to the current object, boundary, evidence, domain, purpose, and conditions.

The principle running through the framework is:

> **No determination acquires unconditional entitlement to continue expanding.**

---

## IV. Principle against illegitimate shortcutting

The framework grows not mainly by "adding answers" but by repeatedly discovering that:

> An earlier answer is not entitled to bear the burden of proof of the next question.

If one wishes to move from `X` to `Y`, but an intermediate responsibility `R` has not yet been discharged:

$$
X \rightarrow R \rightarrow Y
$$

it must not be silently compressed into:

$$
X \rightarrow Y
$$

Only when the current structure actually discharges the intermediate responsibility does the inference acquire entitlement for the current use.

When a responsibility is missing, only four kinds of response are legitimate:

1. **Add a responsibility position:** the current structure genuinely lacks an analytically indispensable responsibility.
2. **Make an explicit handoff:** the question belongs to a domain model, normative theory, law, professional procedure, or another module.
3. **Keep the question open:** there is not yet proportionate grounds, but candidate formation and material acquisition can continue.
4. **Stop determination:** there is no longer entitlement to impose further differentiating determination.

This principle does not guarantee that a conclusion is correct. It only forbids an undischarged responsibility from being presented as already completed.

---

## V. Responsibility is not one thing

"Responsibility" should be separated into at least three different positions:

$$
\text{epistemic / determinative responsibility}
\neq
\text{normative responsibility}
\neq
\text{authority to act}
$$

### 1. Epistemic / determinative responsibility

It asks:

- By what grounds is this determination made?
- How were the materials acquired?
- What are the object and its boundary?
- Which factors were retained or omitted?
- How far can the current structure legitimately infer?
- What evidence would weaken or defeat it?
- When should execution, structure, factors, distinction, validation, or the problem definition be revised?

Finitude and the principle against illegitimate shortcutting constrain this layer first.

### 2. Normative responsibility

It asks:

- What is worth pursuing?
- What ought to be protected or undertaken?
- Whose interests, experiences, commitments, and losses should enter judgment?
- How should conflicts among subjects receive legitimate treatment?

This layer cannot be automatically derived from facts, intelligence, stability, or reliability.

### 3. Authority to act

It asks:

- Who is qualified to give a judgment real-world effect?
- Who decides, executes, verifies, stops, recovers, and exits?
- What are the object, scope, strength, and duration of authority?
- How are third-party risks and non-compensable boundaries handled?

"Able to judge" and "authorized to act" must remain separate.

---

## VI. Two kinds of responsibility integrity

### 1. Integrity of determinative responsibility

**Integrity of determinative responsibility** means that in cognition and inference, every responsibility position that cannot be crossed for free remains explicit. A problem may remain unresolved, but unknowns must not be disguised as knowledge, and a missing responsibility must not be silently replaced by an upstream answer.

It concerns:

$$
\text{acquisition}
\rightarrow
\text{distinction}
\rightarrow
\text{factors}
\rightarrow
\text{organization}
\rightarrow
\text{candidates}
\rightarrow
\text{judgment}
\rightarrow
\text{validation}
\rightarrow
\text{revision}
$$

Integrity does not mean that every step has an algorithm, nor that the candidate space can be exhausted.

### 2. Integrity of shared-affairs and practical responsibility

When determinations enter a reality shared by multiple people, new questions arise that cannot be directly inferred from epistemic responsibility:

- who endorses the purpose;
- who has decision and execution authority;
- who bears benefits, risks, and losses;
- who can express dissent, refuse, stop, or demand review;
- who is responsible for repair, recovery, and residual responsibility;
- how temporary authority exits or is reauthorized.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{integrity of determinative responsibility}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{integrity of shared-affairs and practical responsibility}
}
$$

The latter must be established separately in intersubjective shared affairs.

---

## VII. Handoff integrity and capacity to reopen

The absence of shortcuts inside a module is not enough. Responsibility can also be lost during handoff between modules.

A proportionate handoff should preserve, as far as possible:

- the current conclusion;
- object, scope, and purpose;
- key assumptions;
- evidence and material sources;
- principal unknowns;
- candidates not adopted but still entitled to consideration;
- refutation or rejection conditions;
- current closure state;
- reopen conditions.

Framework integrity therefore has at least three dimensions:

$$
\boxed{
\text{responsibility integrity within modules}
+
\text{handoff integrity}
+
\text{reopenability}
}
$$

If a new problem cannot be adequately described by the existing responsibility positions, the legitimate result is not forced classification. Instead, "the current Responsibility Topology may be inadequate" itself becomes an object of revision.

---

## VIII. Redefining completeness

This framework does not seek:

$$
\text{completeness}=\text{all ultimate questions already have answers}
$$

It is closer to:

$$
\boxed{
\text{completeness}
=
\text{unknowns are not disguised as known}
+
\text{missing responsibilities are not silently substituted}
+
\text{breakpoints know how to hand off, reopen, or stop}
}
$$

Thus, leaving blanks can be compatible with completeness.

The real danger is not "there is no answer here." It is when an easier answer occupies the position that properly belongs to a harder question, making the system believe that the problem has already ended.

Failures can be further distinguished as:

- **answer failure:** the responsibility was discharged, but the conclusion was later rejected by reality;
- **responsibility-omission failure:** a necessary intermediate responsibility existed, but an illegitimate shortcut occurred;
- **responsibility-topology failure:** a new problem exceeded the existing responsibility structure and the system failed to notice that the structure itself needed reopening.

A mature system does not require that failure never occur; it requires failures to appear, as far as possible, at the correct responsibility layer.

---

## IX. Why difficult questions may remain blank

The framework can specify **responsibility positions** with relative completeness while keeping **capability realization** incomplete.

For example:

- how genuinely valuable new candidates are generated;
- where the next unit of cognitive resource should be invested;
- when the current determination has become proportionate;
- whether execution, structure, or the problem definition should be revised;
- which values should ultimately be endorsed;
- which normative arrangements are legitimate.

These positions can be identified, recorded, handed off, and reopened without pretending that an ultimate algorithm already exists.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{responsibility positions can be relatively complete}
\neq
\text{actual capability is already complete}
}
$$

For a harness, these blanks can be routed to the strongest currently available model, expert, or tool. For an individual's internalized structure, they form a long-term cognitive frontier.

## X. Entitlement to continued use and framework self-review

The fact that the current architecture $F$ is adopted does not mean it permanently remains qualified to serve as the responsibility architecture:

$$
\boxed{
F_t\text{ is currently adopted}
\not\Rightarrow
F_t\text{ is permanently entitled to continued use}
}
$$

This section concerns conditions for self-review of the current framework; it does not elevate the language of $F$ into a universal cross-framework ontology.

### 1. Four V1 self-review signals

1. **Failure of responsibility topology:** important problems recur, but existing owners, specializations, or handoffs cannot adequately receive them, and blind spots persist even after local adjustments.
2. **Failure of openness:** the framework repeatedly outputs only `unknown`, `reopen`, `handoff`, or "keep open," yet no longer generates new real distinctions, candidates, acquisition paths, narrowed scopes, reformulated problems, or justified stopping points.
3. **Runaway complexity:** maintaining the current architecture continually requires special owners, modes, exceptions, fields, or patches without producing proportionate new work.
4. **Failure of reflexivity:** the framework requires lower-level definitions, models, evaluators, and governance rules to accept validation, revision, and retirement while exempting the current framework itself from review.

These signals trigger review only; they do not predetermine the result.

### 2. Separating challenger admission from migration

A challenger expressed in another language does not need to be translated into the current $F$'s canonical IDs before it receives standing to challenge:

$$
\boxed{
\text{challenger admission}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{current ontology mapping first}
}
$$

During competition, frameworks may be compared on real consequences, exposure of failures, unknowns, reconstructability of history, and real and institutional constraints without requiring semantic isomorphism. Migration responsibility arises only when a candidate is ready to enter current routing, replace a component, or form a federation. The concrete governance of migration belongs to the [Responsibility Topology Overview](responsibility-topology-overview.md).

### 3. Review outcome

A framework-level review is itself a finite, fallible, reopenable judgment. Its outcomes may include:

$$
\boxed{
\text{framework review}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{retain the current architecture},\\
\text{scope contraction},\\
\text{niche specialization},\\
\text{local or topological reconstruction},\\
\text{component substitution},\\
\text{federation},\\
\text{decomposition},\\
\text{successor supersede},\\
\text{deprecated / archived}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\text{framework review}\not\Rightarrow\text{framework retirement}
$$

### 4. V1 research stopping boundary

Cross-language correspondence, recurrence / irreplaceability, ablation / substitution, architecture-genesis, evaluator sensitivity, and common capture of the research ecology may continue to be studied. They are not completion conditions of Framework V1.0 and do not create new canonical owners in this document.

If such research would only improve explanatory precision without changing public semantics, responsibility boundaries, failure localization, reopenability, or handoff capacity, it remains in the research workspace by default rather than reopening the V1 core.

## XII. General principle

The current framework can be compressed as:

> **A finite system may form fallible determinations only within the entitlement of its currently obtained determinations; stronger epistemic, value, intersubjective, or real-action layers avoid unqualified expansion through explicit differentiation of responsibility, handoff, openness, reopening, and stopping.**

`E₀` provides the limiting discipline:

> **When there is no entitlement to continue determination, do not fill the blank with an answer.**

Framework V1.0 adds one architectural discipline:

> **When there is no necessity in public semantics, responsibility boundaries, or localization of real-world failure, do not continue expanding the core.**

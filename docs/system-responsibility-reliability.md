# System Responsibility Reliability

> **Knowledge and evidence boundary:** This document studies, among multiple finite, fallible, self-modifying, mutually influencing systems, which structures make local error, epistemic closure, concentration of power, concentration of dependency, or technological diffusion less likely to escalate into cross-system, cross-temporal lock-in that is difficult to reopen. It does not define “civilization” as a unified subject, does not prove the ultimate legitimacy of any civilizational goal, and does not substitute reliability diagnosis for law, politics, ethics, safety, engineering, or other domain judgment.

> **Core discipline:** Reliability analysis permits provisional closure, concentrated action, long-term commitments, and path closure, but must ask whether these changes simultaneously destroy the real conditions of observation, dissent, validation, correction, memory, fault containment, recovery, regaining control, and reopening. Higher reliability is not automatically normatively better.

## Definition Dependencies and V1 Public Interface

The V1 public interface includes only **finite civilizational reliability, correction loop, common capture of correction loops, fault containment, rate compatibility, effective reversibility, reauthorization structure, and civilizational maneuverability**.

Observation failure, dissent failure, validation failure, correction failure, memory failure, epistemic lineage, reliability heterogeneity, common-cause containment for recovery, fault domain, irreversible failure surface, reliability profile, and reliability claim may still be used rigorously in the prose, but they are document-local working vocabulary. Naming them does not enlarge the platform-level canonical surface.

Difference, factor, organizational structure, orientation, judgment, action, correction, and open / closure are inherited from the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](02-theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md); candidate generation, cognitive frontier, open validation, revision depth, finite reflexivity, and selective consolidation from the [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](03-theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md); strategic actor and strategic reopen attribution from the [Theory of Interaction Responsibility](04-theory-of-interaction-responsibility.md).

This document answers only **what structural reliability problems should be diagnosed**. Concrete checks, procedure strength, authority, execution, verification, stopping, recovery, and exit are uniformly handed to [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md). This document does not maintain a second operational checklist.

When structural diagnosis enters runtime governance, facts, risk interpretation, and governance action must remain separate. A runtime may use an explicit policy profile for local rate, parallelism, side-effect, blast-radius, exposure, cooldown, and recovery-window thresholds. These numerical values belong to replaceable, versioned profiles, not to this theory or the framework as universal safety invariants. An assessment should be able to answer separately: what was observed (**observation**), what risk is judged from it (**risk assessment**), and what action the current profile requires (**disposition**).

## I. Working Boundary

**Finite civilizational reliability** is the analysis, within a scope of multiple finite systems that depend on and influence one another across time and may share resources, infrastructure, standards, power structures, knowledge structures, and fault domains, of which organizational, dependency, propagation, recovery, authorization, and epistemic structures make local mismatch less likely to escalate into large-scale common error, cross-fault-domain propagation, rapid lock-in, loss of recovery capability, or inability to reopen after fundamental mismatch.

It can be compressed as:

$$
\boxed{
\text{finite civilizational reliability}
=
\text{sustainability of correction loops}
+
\text{containment of lock-in across fault domains}
+
\text{real ability to maneuver again after fundamental mismatch}
}
$$

The “+” indicates three analytical responsibilities that jointly constitute the module. It does not mean they are additive, compensable, or reducible to a total score.

“Civilization” here is only a working analytical scope:

$$
\text{civilizational analytical scope}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unified subject},
$$

$$
\text{civilizational analytical scope}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{single objective function}.
$$

Subject qualification, expressive rights, decision authority, responsibility, and legitimate authority must be judged separately.

## II. Correction Loops

A **correction loop** is a combination of mechanisms through which, as reality advances, a system can still acquire new differences, allow competing explanations to enter, use validation that is not wholly derived from the same source, allow mismatches to change subsequent structure or action, and preserve enough memory to reconstruct the history of judgment.

The minimum functional positions are:

$$
\boxed{
\text{observation}
+
\text{dissent}
+
\text{validation}
+
\text{correction}
+
\text{memory}
}
$$

These positions may be carried by the same organization or distributed across multiple systems. Reliability concerns their real independence, not the number of institutions.

### 1. Five local failure types

| Failure | Minimum meaning |
|---|---|
| Observation failure | Real differences cannot enter the current acquisition structure, or input channels systematically omit critical changes |
| Dissent failure | Alternative explanations, anomalies, or information from affected parties cannot form candidates that enter later judgment |
| Validation failure | The evaluation process shares key data, models, incentives, or sources of power with the evaluated structure, producing highly correlated errors |
| Correction failure | Detected mismatch cannot change models, rules, authority, action, or resource allocation |
| Memory failure | Failures, counterexamples, rejected candidates, old versions, and reasons for revision cannot be reconstructed by later systems |

These failure types are local analytical terms within correction loops and do not individually become V1 public interfaces.

### 2. Common capture of correction loops

**Common capture of correction loops** occurs when multiple critical positions among observation, dissent, validation, correction, and memory share the same failure source, so that multiple apparent stages exist without producing proportionately independent veto by reality.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{formal division of labor}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{fault independence}
}
$$

Multiple institutions, models, or verifiers may still exhibit highly correlated error if they share the same data source, training, identity infrastructure, funding, promotion structure, technological root, metric, or source of authorization.

### 3. Epistemic lineage and reliability heterogeneity

Correction requires being able to reconstruct “why a judgment formed, what vetoes it passed, which alternatives were discarded, and what conditions require reopening.” This provenance and revision history may be called **epistemic lineage**.

Reliability heterogeneity concerns not the number of viewpoints but whether critical failures arise from different sources. A useful working question is whether:

$$
P(B\text{ exhibits the same class of failure}\mid A\text{ has failed})
$$

remains high under the current mechanism. The expression is only a question about conditional dependence, not a universal probability model supplied by this document.

## III. Fault Containment

**Fault containment** means that after a local error, failure, or erroneous control state occurs, real boundaries prevent it from entering other systems, critical resources, and recovery paths without condition; and recovery does not itself depend entirely on the same common cause that produced the original failure.

Distinguish at least three dependency networks:

$$
\mathfrak N^{op},
\qquad
\mathfrak N^{fail},
\qquad
\mathfrak N^{rec},
$$

for normal operation, fault propagation, and recovery respectively. They must not be assumed identical.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
\text{operational redundancy}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{recovery redundancy}
}
$$

Multiple systems may operate independently under normal conditions while, after failure, all depend on the same identity system, keys, supply chain, knowledge base, maintenance team, update channel, or recovery model.

**Recovery common-cause containment** is an internal analytical position within fault containment: when multiple operational systems are damaged together, there remain recovery materials, authority, knowledge, people, infrastructure, or migration paths that do not fully depend on the original source of failure.

A **fault domain** is a set of nodes, roles, or paths that can simultaneously lose critical function because of one shared source of failure. Modular independence, organizational independence, code independence, or legal independence cannot by itself prove fault-domain independence.

## IV. Rate Compatibility

**Rate compatibility** requires the system's capabilities for observation, judgment, coordination, stopping, recovery, and reauthorization to be proportionate to the speeds of error propagation, damage accumulation, and path lock-in.

Let:

- $\tau_o$: time needed to acquire critical differences;
- $\tau_j$: time needed to form a proportionate judgment;
- $\tau_a$: time needed to intervene in reality;
- $\tau_d$: time window in which deviation develops into major or difficult-to-recover damage.

A basic risk position is:

$$
\tau_o+\tau_j+\tau_a
\gtrsim
\tau_d.
$$

This is not a universal safety threshold. It only marks the structural problem in which “correction may be slower than damage.”

Rate compatibility is not “slower is safer.” Excessive slowness may cause persistent harm, resource exhaustion, power vacuums, or missed recovery windows. The question is whether the speed of decision and propagation is proportionate to bandwidth for detection, veto, stopping, rollback, and recovery.

A particularly important distinction is **lock-in speed**: how quickly a decision closes many real paths, unifies infrastructure, changes the distribution of power, or eliminates alternative control entry points. The same final state has a different reliability structure when reached through local trials, asynchronous adoption, and long-lived exit than when deployed globally and synchronously in a short time.

## V. Effective Reversibility

**Effective reversibility** is the existence, after failure, of the real conditions required for relevant subjects or systems to discover the problem, form alternative candidates, regain control, obtain resources, complete migration, and recover the relevant real states.

It differs from formal reversibility in text or interfaces:

$$
\boxed{
\text{formal reversibility}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{effective reversibility}
}
$$

Effective reversibility can be analyzed through three internal failure surfaces:

| Failure surface | Meaning |
|---|---|
| State irreversibility | Real states, ecosystems, bodies, data, relations, or infrastructure can no longer be recovered within proportionate cost and time |
| Control irreversibility | Even when the problem is known, relevant subjects have lost real authority and interfaces for stopping, takeover, migration, modification, or exit |
| Epistemic irreversibility | The knowledge, data, space for dissent, concepts, people, or validation capacity required to form alternative judgments have disappeared |

The three cannot be compressed into a fixed ordinal scale. A system may lose control before state, or lose epistemic capacity first while retaining surface stability for a long time.

Higher-order lock-in is especially concerned with arrangements that not only close current options, but also damage the future ability to generate candidates again, reacquire prerequisites, or rebuild control.

## VI. Reauthorization Structure

A **reauthorization structure** means that high-impact, long-lived, or exceptional authority does not become permanent merely because it was initially granted; continued validity must be reacquired under an explicit scope, duration, evidence basis, and independent review conditions.

Authority is not a Boolean variable. At minimum distinguish object, scope, intensity, duration, termination conditions, and takeover positions.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{once authorized}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{still authorized}
}
$$

Emergency, experimental, or temporary high-authority states are especially prone to becoming permanent through inertia, dependency, sunk cost, or lack of exit paths. Reliability analysis identifies this structural risk only; who may authorize, default state at expiry, legality, and rights boundaries belong to law, institutions, and [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md).

## VII. Civilizational Maneuverability

**Civilizational maneuverability** is the capacity of long-term systems, after fundamental mismatch, environmental change, or failure of existing paths, to form new candidates, enter different real trajectories, bear switching costs, regain control, and, where necessary, rebuild critical prerequisites that have been lost.

It does not mean “more options are always better” and does not require every path to remain permanently open.

It can be analyzed through five internal responsibilities:

1. **Candidate generation:** Are there still sources of exploration and knowledge production not completely controlled by the current dominant structure?
2. **Real reachability:** Do candidates have genuinely executable real-world paths rather than being only conceptually imaginable?
3. **Switching cost:** Does migration from the current structure to an alternative require intolerable loss of resources, time, relations, or rights?
4. **Control entry:** Who can initiate, stop, migrate, take over, or modify critical processes?
5. **Reacquisition:** Can the knowledge, qualifications, tools, resources, and institutional conditions required by closed paths be rebuilt?

Therefore:

$$
\text{more candidates}\not\Rightarrow\text{more real reachability},
$$

$$
\text{formal exit}\not\Rightarrow\text{real exit},
$$

$$
\text{no candidate currently formed}\neq\text{no other possibility exists},
$$

$$
\text{greater maneuverability}\not\Rightarrow\text{normatively better}.
$$

How many paths should be closed, and how much maneuverability should be sacrificed, are normative and domain judgments.

## VIII. Reliability Profile

A concrete system may use a **reliability profile** to describe structural exposures side by side without forming a total score:

| Dimension | Primary question |
|---|---|
| Correction loop | Where do observation, dissent, validation, correction, and memory fail, and is there common capture? |
| Fault containment | Do normal, failure, and recovery networks share critical common causes? |
| Rate compatibility | Can correction bandwidth keep up with propagation, damage, and lock-in speed? |
| Effective reversibility | Can state, control, and epistemic capability still be recovered? |
| Reauthorization | Do high-authority states have expiry, independent review, and real termination entry points? |
| Maneuverability | Do new candidates, alternative paths, control entry points, and reacquisition capacity still exist? |

The purpose of the profile is to answer “how might this system fail?” not “what is this system's total score?” Dimensions cannot automatically compensate one another through weighted averaging.

## IX. Tension Between Coordination Capacity and Reliability

Concentration, standardization, synchronization, unified interfaces, and long-term commitments may improve coordination capacity while increasing common-mode failure, propagation speed, and common-cause recovery risk. Conversely, excessive fragmentation can also break important cooperation, resource concentration, and crisis response.

This document therefore does not presuppose universal optimality of concentration or decentralization, openness or closure, uniformity or heterogeneity. Reliability analysis requires stating what current coordination benefits depend on and what alternatives, stopping, recovery, and reauthorization capacities remain if failure occurs.

## X. Open, Closure, and Reliability Reopening

Reliability questions may be provisionally closed to support design and action, but closure must state object, boundary, time scale, purpose, and residual unknowns.

The following changes may trigger reopening:

- critical observation, validation, or recovery paths exhibit common failure;
- error propagation or lock-in speed exceeds prior estimates;
- formal backup, rollback, or exit proves unusable in real failure;
- high-authority states cannot be terminated or taken over as planned;
- new dependencies, concentration points, or recovery common causes emerge;
- previously reachable alternatives close because prerequisites disappear;
- new candidates or anomalous outcomes reveal omissions in the existing reliability profile.

On reopening, first judge whether the answer, evidence, model, system boundary, or whole problem definition needs revision. Concrete revision procedures are handed to [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md).

## XI. Normative Boundary and Practice Handoff

Reliability analysis may factually indicate that an arrangement increases common capture, fault propagation, lock-in, irrecoverability, or loss of future maneuverability, but it cannot automatically derive “must prohibit,” “must preserve openness,” or “this institution is more legitimate.”

Maintain:

$$
\text{higher reliability}\not\Rightarrow\text{normatively better},
$$

$$
\text{high reliability risk}\not\Rightarrow\text{automatic prohibition},
$$

$$
\text{conditions for survival or openness}\not\Rightarrow\text{universal foundation of value}.
$$

When entering real-world action:

- procedure strength, authority, third-party boundaries, execution, verification, recovery, and exit are handed to [Action Responsibility Practice](08-action-responsibility-practice.md);
- subject experience, individual value commitments, and value-conflict boundaries are handed to the [Theory of Value Responsibility](05-theory-of-value-responsibility.md);
- rights, obligations, legality, and institutional legitimacy are handed to legal and normative procedures;
- mechanisms, probabilities, thresholds, damage, and recovery costs are handed to domain models.

This document no longer maintains operational checklists such as “what must at least be checked if backups exist” or “minimum fields in a diagnostic record.” Those belong to the practice layer.

## XII. Central Proposition

The core of finite civilizational reliability is not to seek a system that is always open, never concentrated, and free of commitments, but:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{permit finite systems to provisionally close and act in reality},\\
&\text{while preventing local error, power, or dependency from completing irreopenable lock-in before correction},\\
&\text{and preserve conditions for forming new candidates, regaining control, and entering other real trajectories after fundamental mismatch}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

This document supplies those diagnostic dimensions. What action to take, with what intensity, and who has authority to act must continue to be handed off.

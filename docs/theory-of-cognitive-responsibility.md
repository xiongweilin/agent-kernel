# Theory of Cognitive Responsibility

> **Knowledge and evidence boundary:** This document specializes the finite-system intervention layer of the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md). It explains how finite systems form, invoke, test, and reorganize usable structures, and how they selectively allocate cognitive activity when neither reality nor unrealized possibilities can be completely acquired or exhaustively unfolded. Its boundaries for intelligence, agency, and subjectivity are a working original synthesis; concrete mechanisms of perception, attention, memory, affect, learning, neuroscience, animal cognition, and artificial systems remain matters for domain evidence.

## Definition Dependencies

The V1 public interface includes finite intelligence, candidate generation, structural tension, directional revision candidates, epistemic action, material acquisition, open validation, revision-depth judgment, finite reflexivity, counterfactual construction, cognitive-search-allocation judgment, cognitive frontier, selective consolidation, functional agency, and the working boundary of subjectivity. Other named processes and working notation belong to the internal vocabulary of this document and may be revised without changing the cross-document interface.

`E₀`, difference, retention, distinction, factor, organizational structure, orientation, judgment, action, correction, and open / closure are inherited from the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md). When persistent recording of current support, versions, or processing state is required, responsibility is handed to the [Responsibility Record Plane](responsibility-record-plane.md), but this theory does not import its record tokens. This document specializes mechanisms only; it does not establish a second meta-model, normative legitimacy, an action procedure, or a record schema.

## I. Working Definitions and System Boundary

**Finite intelligence** is the capacity of a finite system, within an explicit system boundary, to form or acquire usable distinctions, factors, and organizational structures; to invoke them in relevant situations; and, to some degree, to revise them on the basis of experience and real-world results, thereby supporting discrimination, prediction, judgment, selection, or action.

When facing open tasks in which the structure itself may need revision, a more mature finite intelligence can additionally form and manage candidates, judge determination, joint compatibility, cross-context correspondence, and real-world testability, allocate epistemic activity, and use open validation to judge the depth of revision.

**Intelligence generation** is the process by which inherited or preconfigured structures, direct experience, structured teaching, and other connections to reality cause usable structures to form.

**Intelligent activity** is the process by which a system invokes existing structures in a concrete situation, actively acquires relevant differences, organizes candidates, performs proportionate search, judgment, and action, and corrects itself through outcomes.

A **callable structure** is an internal or distributed structure that can be retrieved and combined in an appropriate situation and can actually enter prediction, judgment, or action. Stored content that cannot enter subsequent activity does not thereby constitute currently usable intelligence.

Finite intelligence is not defined solely by “how many tasks can be completed,” nor is it identical with subjectivity, power, responsibility, or legitimate authority.

This document inherits the analytical main chain:

```text
Reality → Distinction → Factors → Organizational Structure → Judgment → Action → New Reality
```

A finite system enters each round already carrying prior organizational structure, orientation, history, capabilities, and channels. Therefore:

$$
S,G\rightarrow D,
\qquad
F,S,G\rightarrow J
$$

Usable structure may be distributed across the body, memory, sensors, text, tools, models, databases, organizational institutions, and collaborative relations. Attribution of capability must match the actual system boundary: if training, maintenance, or human revision occurs in a larger system, that revisability cannot be directly attributed to a narrower deployment instance whose parameters are fixed.

## II. How Structures Form and Enter Use

### 1. Sources of structure

Finite systems usually do not begin from a blank state. Usable structure can arise from at least:

1. **Inheritance or preconfiguration:** biological evolution, bodily structure, cultural traditions, technical design, training processes, and existing institutions provide initial classifications, capabilities, and constraints;
2. **Direct experience:** perception, action, outcomes, and feedback change existing structures;
3. **Structured teaching:** other subjects, communities, or technical systems arrange differences, sequences, examples, and feedback so that the learner reconstructs usable structure under its own conditions.

The three enter one another: existing structure changes what the system can notice and interpret, while direct experience subjects inherited or taught structure to renewed constraint from reality.

### 2. From difference to callable structure

From real-world differences to structures capable of supporting intelligent activity, at least five functional positions are required:

1. **Connection to reality and input-accessible difference:** real differences must be able to enter current processing through the current body, perception, sensors, media, records, databases, other subjects, experimental apparatus, or other real connections. Modality, coverage, resolution, sampling, noise, transformation, and authority constrain what can be acquired. This establishes only that a condition of connection to reality exists; it does not require every cycle to represent acquisition independently as $\mathcal Q$.
2. **Distinction that forms factors:** under the current boundary, scale, purpose, existing structure, and orientation, the system separates, merges, compares, locates, and stabilizes accessible content so that some of it becomes factors $F$ able to enter later organization, judgment, or action.
3. **Organization:** factors form classifications, relations, sequences, causal candidates, values, orientations, authority structures, or other organization. The organizing process is influenced by existing structure, orientation, history, and conditions:

$$
\mathcal O_t
\mathcal O_t =
\mathcal O(F_t\mid S_t^{-},G_t^{-},\mathcal H_t,C_t,\ldots)
$$

Thus different systems may form different organizational candidates from the same factors; differences between expert and novice can occur in both capacity for distinction and capacity for organization.
4. **Invocation:** stored structures can be retrieved and combined in appropriate situations; storage does not equal actual usability.
5. **Constraint from reality:** structures enter prediction, explanation, planning, or action, acquire materials through real connections, and are then assessed through validation for how those materials constrain the current structure. Where necessary, parameters, structures, object boundaries, factor sets, acquisition methods, validation methods, or orientations are changed.

This can be compressed as:

$$
S,G\rightarrow D\rightarrow F\xrightarrow{\mathcal O}S'\rightarrow J/A
$$

The expression marks only functional positions. It does not claim a mechanically linear temporal sequence, nor does it assume that $F$ is a complete factor set independent of organizational structure.

### 3. Active distinction and orientation

**Active distinction** does not create real differences by will. It means that, under existing structure and orientation, a system changes how real inputs enter processing or how they are distinguished. Changing observation position, channel, apparatus, data source, or sampling conditions primarily changes the connection to reality or mode of material acquisition; changing the object boundary, scale, comparison standard, search space, classification granularity, or merge rule while inputs remain broadly similar primarily changes distinction $D$. Both may change together, but a changed factor set $F$ must not be used to conflate “the acquired content changed” with “the mode of distinction changed.”

Orientation can change the salience of differences, search direction, factor formation, structure invocation, and the priority of candidate actions. The legitimacy of purposes and authorization are not judged by this document.

### 4. Distributed structure and migration of mapping loss

High-dimensional or machine-native inputs may bypass some re-encoding designed for human senses, but this does not eliminate finitude. This document calls a shift in where loss occurs after a channel or representation change **migration of mapping loss**. Common sources include sampling and quantization, irreversible compression, noise and drift, incorrect correspondences across objects or time, selective omissions introduced by training objectives and evaluators, retrieval and routing failures, and attention to only local outputs while neglecting real-world consequences.

Different losses require different evidence and correction mechanisms; they cannot all be written as “information reduction.” Bypassing human-readable intermediate representations may increase processing capability while decreasing explainability, dissent, auditability, and responsibility traceability.

## III. Learning, Reorganization, and Incomplete Candidate Spaces

**Learning** is a process in which experience produces relatively persistent changes in later distinctions, factors, organizational structures, invocation, judgments, or actions. Change does not guarantee greater correctness or cross-context transfer, and adaptation within a single context cannot automatically be classified as persistent learning.

**Structural reorganization** is correction that goes beyond parameters or local relations and changes object boundaries, factor sets, classifications, state spaces, principal organizational relations, invocation methods, material acquisition methods, validation methods, orientations, or problem definitions.

Distinguish:

- **within-model unknown:** objects, variables, states, and candidate relations have been specified, but evidence is insufficient;
- **incomplete candidate space:** the current object types, factor set, classification, state dimensions, relation forms, system boundary, mode of combination, or problem structure may itself omit relevant possibilities.

An incomplete candidate space does not mean “anything is possible” and does not require assuming that reality contains a possibility set which the system could ultimately enumerate completely. Anomalies provide material for reopening and comparing alternative explanations; they do not directly prove that the current space must be incomplete.

An important form of structural reorganization is **joint-structure reorganization**: revising which local determinations may enter the same joint problem, how they are combined, and which must remain conditional, separate, or cannot simultaneously be assigned. It is not a new top-level capacity, only one form of structural reorganization.

## IV. Selective Possibility Management

### 1. Candidate generation does not presuppose a complete space

Finite intelligence need not first obtain a complete candidate space and then search it. **Candidate generation** means that, under currently callable structure, the problem, conditions, history, and generation mechanism, a candidate is actually formed that can continue to be processed. Working notation:

$$
\mathsf{Gen}_t(K_t,q,\xi)\rightsquigarrow h
$$

where $K_t$ denotes currently callable structure, $q$ the current problem, $\xi$ conditions or perturbations relevant to generation, and $h$ the candidate actually formed. The notation does not presuppose that all candidates form a known set.

This document retains the name **principle of inexhaustible possibility**, but its minimum meaning is only that a finite system cannot infer a complete candidate totality from the candidates currently formed, nor presuppose that it can exhaustively unfold every relevant candidate not yet formed. It is not an ontological claim about the number of possibilities in reality.

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\text{candidate generation does not presuppose a complete candidate space}
}
$$

A finite system can directly process, compare, and evaluate only candidates that have actually formed and currently qualify for processing. It cannot infer from them a complete, fully acquired, in-principle enumerable, or system-created totality of real possibilities.

### 2. Cognitive frontier and selective possibility management

**Selective possibility management** is the higher-order activity by which a finite system, unable to completely acquire reality and unable to presuppose a complete candidate totality, generates, unfolds, compares, temporarily stores, prunes, reality-tests, and reopens candidates that have actually formed.

Let $\mathcal F_t$ denote the current **cognitive frontier**:

$$
\mathcal F_t
\mathcal F_t =
\{h_i\mid h_i\text{ has actually formed and currently remains eligible for refinement, unfolding, comparison, testing, holding, or reopening}\}
$$

$h_i$ may be a variable direction, scale, object boundary, representation, action, observation method, validation standard, interpretation of goals, or other low-commitment candidate; $\mathcal F_t$ is a current working set, not the set of all possibilities.

The basic epistemic discipline is:

$$
\text{not generated}\neq\text{nonexistent},
\qquad
\text{currently unconstructible}\neq\text{impossible},
\qquad
\text{pruned}\neq\text{impossible},
\qquad
\text{provisionally closed}\neq\text{the problem is ended}
$$

### 3. Higher-order responsibilities for determination, joint compatibility, and real-world testability

After a candidate is formed, a finite system still needs to judge distinct epistemic responsibilities. These judgments are not treated as oracles that return truth from a God's-eye view. Judgments whose objects are current determination, joint compatibility, real-world testability, cognitive-search allocation, and revision activity itself are collectively called **higher-order judgments**. $J_{\mathrm{meta}}$ is only a convenient group label when compressed notation is needed; it does not denote an independent subject, module, unified algorithm, or final validation source.

Working notation may include:

$$
J^{\mathrm{def}}_{t,\mathcal D,C}(h)
$$

which judges whether candidate $h$ is sufficiently determinate under the current domain model $\mathcal D$, boundary, and conditions $C$;

$$
J^{\mathrm{comp}}_{t,\mathcal D,C}(H)
$$

which judges whether a set of separately determinable candidates $H$ has sufficient reason to enter the same joint structure and be jointly retained;

$$
J^{\mathrm{det}}_{t,\mathcal D,C}(H,S^\ast)
$$

which judges whether, even if those local determinations can be combined, they are already sufficient to determine the joint structure $S^\ast$ presently under discussion;

$$
J^{\mathrm{op}}_{t,\mathcal D,C}(h)
$$

which judges whether, under current conditions, an adequate real-world arrangement exists through which candidate $h$ or differences among candidates can enter observation, testing, or intervention.

Cross-context comparison also requires an explicit retention standard for correspondence. Working notation:

$$
\iota^{K}_{C_i\rightarrow C_j}:
X_{C_i}
\rightsquigarrow
X_{C_j}
$$

This denotes the current correspondence, under retention standard $K$, by which acquisitions in two contexts are judged identical, equivalent, comparable, or transferable. It is not unconditional equality and not a new meta-model primitive.

These judgments should at minimum preserve current status, reasons, scope, and unknowns. “Undefined,” “incompatible,” “insufficiently determined,” and “currently not real-world-testable” must not be collapsed into one generic state of “insufficient information.”

They are all fallible higher-order judgments and inherit two disciplines from the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md):

$$
\text{separately determinable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{jointly determinable}
$$

$$
\text{jointly determinable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{local representation is sufficient to determine the joint structure}
$$

### 4. Structural tension `T`

**Structural tension** is a relation of unresolved incoherence, still significant for judgment, between currently callable distinctions, factors, organization, orientation, judgment, material-acquisition methods, or validation methods and either constraints from reality or constraints internal to the system.

It may arise from persistent predictive or explanatory residuals, action failure, anomalous success, unexpected regularity, proliferating exceptions, scale conflict, or unstable validation, and may also arise from important internal incoherence among multiple structures, orientations, self-models, or resource requirements. A single anomaly does not automatically prove that a structure is wrong.

### 5. Directional revision candidate `H`

A **directional revision candidate** is a low-commitment candidate for “where we may need to look again, distinguish again, reorganize again, acquire materials again, validate again, or reorient” when the old structure has shown itself insufficient but a new structure is not yet mature.

It may be only a new variable, scale, object boundary, mode of combination, mechanism direction, representation, measurement or sampling method, validation method, goal interpretation, or problem definition. Its role is to form a small number of directions worth distinguishing further, not to prematurely write an insight as a mature structure.

$H$ denotes a directional type of revision candidate, not the candidate working set. A concrete candidate enters the current cognitive frontier $\mathcal F_t$ only after it has actually formed and qualified for continued processing.

### 6. Counterfactual construction $\Phi$

**Counterfactual construction** is the process by which a system, using its currently callable structure, temporarily changes or releases some retention conditions and forms comparable candidate unfoldings of unrealized factors, organizations, actions, orientations, or responses by others.

By object it may be written $\Phi_A$, $\Phi_F$, $\Phi_S$, $\Phi_G$, and so on. More importantly, counterfactual construction is itself constrained by the current organizational structure:

$$
\Phi_{S_t}
$$

This indicates that what can be constructed and which combinations currently make sense may change with $S_t$. Counterfactual construction is not brute-force enumeration over a Cartesian product presupposed to be complete.

Counterfactuals can generate candidates that were not previously represented explicitly, but they cannot replace real-world evidence:

$$
\hat R\neq R
$$

The ability to construct a new orientation $G'$ also does not grant authority to endorse or implement it.

### 7. Cognitive-search-allocation judgment $\Lambda$

**Cognitive-search-allocation judgment** is a revisable higher-order judgment by which a finite system, under current structural tension, candidates, self-model, resources, risk, time, and real-world conditions, decides where to place the next increment of cognitive effort, how much effort to invest, and when to stop.

$\Lambda$ concerns the opportunity value of epistemic activity: how likely continued generation, unfolding, fact-checking, tool use, or experimentation is to change the current judgment, preserve critical options, or expose structural error, relative to time, resources, risk, and opportunity cost.

For cognitive investment across time, one should also check whether the current investment changes the future feasible set for learning, judgment, and action. Some investment is valuable not because it immediately changes a current judgment, but because it builds callable structure that requires long accumulation, or identifies external prerequisites that must be acquired within a time window, thereby preserving paths that would later be difficult to reopen or reducing switching cost. Conversely, maintaining a cognitive direction over time also has maintenance and opportunity costs. Qualifications, relations, records, institutional entry points, and similar items are external real-world conditions rather than cognitive structures themselves; their accumulation mechanisms, sensitive windows, qualification requirements, and irreversible conditions are owned by the relevant domain evidence and [Action Responsibility Practice](action-responsibility-practice.md), not presupposed by this theory.

$\Lambda$ can determine which candidates to continue forming, which deserve $\Phi$, when to consult sources or invoke tools, which unknowns require real-world exploration, which directions to hold or prune, and when further search is unlikely to change the current selection. Therefore, **stopping search can itself be intelligent behavior**.

$\Lambda$ is fallible. Search may stop too early, while continued search may waste resources or delay action; later outcomes, candidates not previously generated, dissent, or real change may all reopen evaluation of the earlier search allocation. When continuing search—or continuing to inspect the current higher-order judgment itself—is unlikely to change the current selection, and the time, resource, risk, and opportunity costs have become disproportionate, a finite system may provisionally stop reflexive inspection. That stopping creates only current closure, not final correctness, and should preserve key unknowns, routes of refutation, and reopening conditions.

The provisional closure formed here is first an **epistemic-work closure**: existing candidates, structures, and evidence are sufficient for current comparison, derivation, candidate selection, or handoff to a more closed solving process. It does not automatically create authorization for real-world action. Whether an action may be implemented still requires independent judgment about purpose, authority, risk, third-party boundaries, recovery, and stopping conditions. The responsibility boundary is therefore: $\boxed{\text{When has continued cognitive search become disproportionate?}}$ (Finite Intelligence) versus $\boxed{\text{Even if cognitive search may stop, may reality be acted on now?}}$ (Finite Change Practice).

### 8. Epistemic action $A^e$, material acquisition $\mathcal Q$, and open validation

**Epistemic action (exploratory action)** is a specialization of action whose primary purpose is not immediate attainment of a target state but arranging a new encounter with reality to obtain real differences that discriminate among current candidates. Its aim is not maximal information in the abstract, but discriminative information under proportionate cost, risk, and authority.

Counterfactual construction and epistemic action have different epistemic status:

$$
\Phi_{S_t}
\rightarrow
A_t^e
$$

The former forms internal candidates; the latter changes how reality is queried. Neither is itself evidence.

This document denotes the mechanism position of **material acquisition** as $\mathcal Q_t$:

$$
\mathcal Q_t:
(R_t,C_t,\text{channel}_t,\text{apparatus}_t,\ldots)
\rightarrow
E_t^{\mathrm{mat}}
$$

$E_t^{\mathrm{mat}}$ denotes material obtained through current observation, measurement, sampling, logs, interviews, sensing, retrieval, experimental apparatus, or other real connections. $\mathcal Q_t$ is not a universal preprocessor that everything must pass through before distinction, nor is it the only symbol for reality entering an intelligent system. It becomes an explicit analytical object only when how real-world input was obtained itself affects evidential force and needs active arrangement, comparison, audit, or revision. Epistemic action $A_t^e$ may change or arrange the objects, channels, apparatus, sampling, and conditions of $\mathcal Q_t$, but is not a necessary precursor to all material acquisition. $\mathcal Q_t$ may itself suffer from limited coverage, noise, calibration errors, sampling problems, correspondence errors, authority restrictions, and data-pipeline problems.

$\mathcal Q$ and distinction $D$ must remain separable:

$$
\boxed{\text{the same }\mathcal Q\text{ can support different }D}
$$

$$
\boxed{\text{similar }D\text{ can be supported by different }\mathcal Q}
$$

The former means that the same acquisition method can form different factors under different object boundaries, scales, comparison standards, or classification rules. The latter means that similar distinction goals can be supported by different observations, measurements, data sources, or connections to reality. When the resulting $F$ changes, check separately whether the acquired content changed and whether the mode of distinction changed.

**Open validation** $V_{\mathrm{open}}$ no longer carries material acquisition at the same time. While object boundaries, factor sets, principal organizational structures, orientations, or success criteria may still change, it judges what current evidential constraint the acquired material places on candidate structures:

$$
\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{test}}\subseteq\mathcal F_t,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{open}}:
(E_t^{\mathrm{mat}},\mathcal Q_t,D_t,F_t,\mathcal O_t,S_t,\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{test}},\mathcal D_t,\ldots)
\rightarrow
J_t^E
$$

$\mathcal F_t^{\mathrm{test}}$ denotes the subset of candidates actually targeted by the current material; $J_t^E$ may express the material's current support, weakening, discrimination, or unresolved status regarding those candidates. Evidential scope must not be unconditionally extrapolated to the entire cognitive frontier. The basic discipline is:

$$
\boxed{
E_t^{\mathrm{mat}}
\neq
J_t^E
}
$$

That is, **evidence material is not evidential force**. Possessing material does not mean its constraining significance has been judged. How material was acquired, distinguished into factors, and organized can all change the evidential judgment, so $\mathcal Q/D/F/\mathcal O/S$ should remain auditable where relevant.

Open validation should preserve reality's veto routes as far as possible: in addition to what supports the current structure, state what would reduce confidence, narrow the boundary, or force abandonment. When object and standards have been proportionately fixed, closed verification may be used; the two cannot substitute for one another.

### 9. Revision-depth judgment $\Gamma$

**Revision-depth judgment** asks: after a deviation, anomaly, or new regularity appears, which layer of the cognition–action chain should be changed, and how deeply?

$$
\Gamma(E_t^{\mathrm{mat}},J_t^E,T,H,M_{\mathrm{self}},\ldots)
\rightarrow
\text{judgment over possible revision layers and their confidence}
$$

Candidate layers include execution, current judgment, organizing process $\mathcal O$, organizational structure, factor set, mode of distinction, orientation, material acquisition $\mathcal Q$, open validation $V$, self-model, or problem definition. Avoid:

- **revision that is too shallow:** continually adding local patches on top of a failed structure or distorted acquisition chain;
- **revision that is too deep:** escalating local noise, a single anomaly, or an acquisition error into a crisis of the whole structure.

$\Gamma$ and $\Lambda$ are both fallible higher-order judgments: the former decides which layer is most worth doubting; the latter decides where to place the next unit of cognitive effort. $\Gamma$ itself can be revised by later results: if a global reconstruction later proves to have been only a material-acquisition failure, the prior revision was too deep; if repeated local patches leave the same residuals recurring, the prior revision was too shallow.

### 10. Finite reflexivity of higher-order judgment

Higher-order judgments can themselves become objects of later judgment, but this does not require an infinite number of simultaneously existing meta-levels. Earlier determination judgments, compatibility judgments, search allocation $\Lambda$, or revision-depth judgments $\Gamma$ can all be reevaluated in light of later materials, results, dissent, or new candidates. This invokes the same finite system judging again in a new situation, rather than creating an independent $J_{\mathrm{meta-meta}}$, $\Gamma_{\Gamma}$, or other final controller.

$$
\boxed{
\text{reflexive revisability}
\neq
\text{a need for infinitely many simultaneous meta-levels}
}
$$

Reflexivity is implemented through repeated invocation over time. Finite reflexive inspection may provisionally stop at a proportionate closure for current work, but it must preserve residual unknowns, routes of refutation, and reopening conditions; later reality may make the earlier stopping judgment itself an object of revision. **Provisionally stopping reflexive inspection cannot itself constitute action authority.**

### 11. Cognitive structure-genesis cycle

The **cognitive structure-genesis cycle** describes how candidates form, undergo joint and real-world constraint, and gradually enter reorganization or consolidation after old structures no longer adequately absorb real-world or internal constraints. The principal responsibility positions may be expressed as:

$$
T
\rightarrow
\mathsf{Gen}
\rightarrow
h,
\qquad
h\in\mathcal F_t\ \text{iff it currently qualifies for continued processing}
$$

The current cognitive frontier then repeatedly couples to higher-order judgment and exploratory activity:

$$
\mathcal F_t
\leftrightarrow
\{
\Phi_{S_t},\Lambda,
J^{\mathrm{def}},J^{\mathrm{comp}},J^{\mathrm{det}},J^{\mathrm{op}},
\text{search},\text{tool},A^e
\}
$$

When real-world material is required:

$$
\mathcal Q
\rightarrow
E^{\mathrm{mat}}
\rightarrow
V_{\mathrm{open}}
\rightarrow
J^E
\rightarrow
\Gamma
\rightarrow
\text{reorganization, consolidation, or reopening}
$$

$H$ is a candidate type rather than a working state alongside $\mathcal F_t$; $A^e$ can rearrange $\mathcal Q$, while $\mathcal Q$ may also enter directly through existing logs, routine observation, archives, or other real connections. These relations represent responsibility dependencies and common couplings, not a fixed execution order. Positions may be skipped, parallelized, repeatedly invoked, or jointly carried by bodies, people, models, tools, experimental apparatus, databases, and institutions.

When $\Gamma$ supports deeper revision, the system may modify $D/F/\mathcal O/S/G/J/A/\mathcal Q/V$, its self-model, or its problem definition, including through joint-structure reorganization. Open / closure here appears as continued candidate generation, unfolding, holding, pruning, provisional closure, and reopening by new differences.

## V. Maturation of New Structures and Selective Consolidation

### 1. Maturation of a new structure

A candidate structure does not become callable structure merely because it can retrospectively explain existing material. It can be checked from five non-substitutable perspectives:

- **Compression:** Does it reduce the number of independent exceptions or coincidences that must be separately assumed?
- **Prediction:** Does it produce new expectations that can accept constraint from reality?
- **Transfer:** Can it be reconstructed or invoked in nearby new situations?
- **Intervention:** Can it guide how to detect, change, or control relevant reality?
- **Boundary:** Does it know when it should no longer be invoked after conditions, scale, object, or environment change?

These are evaluation perspectives only. They do not form a single scoring function and are not sufficient conditions in any domain.

### 2. Selective consolidation

**Selective consolidation** is the process by which structures worth retaining from experience, together with their applicability boundaries, formation conditions, failure information, and reopening conditions, enter future callable structure and generation tendencies in a proportionate way.

It does not permanently freeze one success. Long-term retention, current-scope retention, decay, reactivation, revalidation, and retirement should be distinguished, while necessary **negative knowledge** is preserved: rejected candidates, failed paths, counterexamples, verifier defects, boundaries of overgeneralization, incorrect attributions, and reopening conditions.

$\Pi_t$ may be used as a convenient aggregate notation for mechanism tendencies participating in intelligence generation and activity at a given time. It is not a complete state vector and does not promise that its components are complete, independent, measurable, share a common dimension, or can be directly updated numerically. Whether corresponding mechanisms exist in a particular system and how they change remains a matter for domain evidence.

What needs to be preserved is this: experience can change not only the content of stored structure, but also how the system later acquires differences, forms factors, organizes relations, generates candidates and counterfactuals, allocates search, acquires materials, judges evidential force, and estimates revision depth. As working notation:

$$
\Pi_t
\leadsto
\Pi_{t+1}
$$

The arrow only indicates that mechanism tendencies may change over time with experience and real-world outcomes. It does not state a numerical state-update equation.

Structured teaching and public externalization may both participate in consolidation and invocation, but neither removes the need for reality-testing. Where community acceptance, authorization, maintenance, succession, dissent, and retirement are involved, hand off to governance questions in the [Responsibility Structure Candidate Catalog](responsibility-structure-candidate-catalog.md) and the relevant domain model.

## VI. Working Boundaries Among Intelligence, Agency, and Subjectivity

At minimum distinguish:

| Form | Primary characteristic | Does not automatically imply |
|---|---|---|
| Reaction | Input difference elicits relatively stable output | Learning, goal endorsement |
| Regulation | Feedback changes a process and maintains a standard | Learning, subjectivity |
| Functional self-maintenance | State and action are coordinated around system boundaries and persistence references | First-person experience, normative legitimacy |
| Learning | Experience persistently changes later distinction, structure, or action | Greater correctness, transferability |
| **Functional agency** | Stored state, orientation, and callable structure actually mediate judgment or selection among multiple real feasible interventions | Subjectivity, qualification for responsibility |
| Finite intelligence | Forms, invokes, and revises usable structures; selectively forms, tests, and reorganizes candidates; remains subject to correction by reality | Subjectivity, authority, legitimacy |
| Subjectivity | Whether a system has its own experience, boundaries, endorsement of orientation, agency, and corresponding continuity | Power over others, all responsibility, irreplaceability |

### 1. Working analytical boundary of subjectivity

This document does not bind the subject in advance to a human substrate, nor does it affirm or deny subject status merely because the substrate is artificial, animal, collective, or otherwise. First-person language, a self-model, goal representation, reward signals, simulated emotion, persistent memory, or complex action cannot individually prove subjectivity; absence of linguistic expression cannot individually disprove it either.

This document owns only the working definition of “how subjectivity should be analyzed in this framework,” not final judgment over real objects. First-person experience, personal identity, and concrete determinations of subjectivity require more specialized mechanisms, evidence, and normative procedures.

### 2. Irreplaceability grounded in concrete causal–relational history

Subject status alone does not imply that “a subject is necessarily irreplaceable by another subject.” A more robust working form of irreplaceability is understood here as **continuity of a concrete causal continuity and concrete relational history**: another instance may have similar properties, functions, memory content, or roles, yet does not thereby become this very instance that underwent this particular causal history and chain of relations.

Properties can be copied, functions can be taken over, roles can be inherited; a concrete history cannot be retroactively written by another instance. This document does not resolve sufficient criteria for copying, branching, memory transfer, or numerical identity.

### 3. Separation of intelligence, power, and legitimacy

Greater intelligence means stronger capacity for structure formation, invocation, and correction. It does not automatically grant the power to set goals, decide, execute, stop, or assign responsibility. Analysis of power and legitimacy belongs to the shared-affairs and normative boundaries of the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md), [Action Responsibility Practice](action-responsibility-practice.md), and concrete institutions.

## VII. Handoff to Empirical Mechanisms and Domains

Attention, memory, affect, heuristics, habits, neural mechanisms, teaching effects, animal perception, and artificial-model architectures may implement or limit the functions described here, but they are not meta-definitions in this document. Whether such mechanisms hold, and in what direction or strength, must be supported by evidence in the corresponding domain.

When evidence, assertions, decisions, actions, outcomes, and revisions require persistent recording, hand off to the [Responsibility Record Plane](responsibility-record-plane.md). For how actions acquire procedural strength and enter reality, see [Action Responsibility Practice](action-responsibility-practice.md).

# Theory of Value Responsibility

> **Knowledge and evidence boundary:** This document specializes possible endogenous sources of orientation `G` in the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md). It explains how real differences form system relevance and regulatory polarity within finite systems, and—only for objects that there is already reason to treat as experiencing subjects—how experiential polarity, first-order orientation, reflective endorsement, and individual value commitment may further form. This document does not derive norms from facts, does not explain how consciousness is generated, does not determine whether a concrete object actually has subjective experience, and does not establish an ultimate good or universal ethics.

> **Core discipline:** A system being affected is not value; regulation is not experience; experience is not an ought; individual endorsement is not a legitimate constraint on others.

## Definition Dependencies and V1 Public Interface

This document has only four public interface items: **finite value genesis, experience-status gate, individual value commitment, and reopening under value conflict**. Consequence difference, system relevance, regulatory polarity, experiential polarity, subject-relative self-involvement, first-order orientation, reflective endorsement, and value-source tracing are still used rigorously in the prose but belong to document-local working vocabulary. Adjusting these auxiliary layers should not automatically trigger cross-document compatibility changes.

`E₀`, `E₁`, distinction, factor, organization, orientation `G`, judgment, action, correction, and open / closure are inherited from the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md). Candidate generation, structural tension, counterfactual construction, open validation, revision depth, finite reflexivity, functional agency, and the working boundary of subjectivity are inherited from the [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md). Authority, risk, verification, recovery, stopping, and exit in real-world action are handed to [Action Responsibility Practice](action-responsibility-practice.md).

This document must not redefine `G`; it answers only: **which reality–system–subject relations can become endogenous sources of `G`, and what epistemic and normative status do those sources have?**

## I. Working Structure of Finite Value Genesis

In the main chain of the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md), orientation satisfies:

$$
G_t\subseteq S_t.
$$

This document unfolds only some endogenous sources:

$$
R
\rightsquigarrow
L_0
\rightsquigarrow
L_1
\rightsquigarrow
L_2
\rightsquigarrow
G.
$$

Only after an object has passed the experience-status gate do we further analyze:

$$
L_2
\dashrightarrow
L_{3a}
\rightsquigarrow
L_{3b}
\rightsquigarrow
L_4
\rightsquigarrow
C_i^{\mathrm{value}}.
$$

Here `\dashrightarrow` means that additional domain evidence concerning subjective experience must be obtained; the step cannot be crossed directly from functional structure.

**Finite value genesis** is the process in which some real differences first form system relevance and regulatory polarity under an explicit system boundary, acquisition conditions, organization, and history; and, for objects there is already reason to treat as experiencing subjects, some of those differences further enter the subject's experience, first-order orientation, and reflective endorsement, thereby forming current individual value commitments.

It is neither a derivation of norms from facts nor a discovery of ultimate value.

## II. Genesis Ladder

| Level | Working term | Minimum meaning | Must not automatically imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| $L_0$ | Consequence difference | Different inputs, states, relations, or actions produce distinguishable differences in subsequent reality | A system preference, value superiority, or a sufficient causal mechanism |
| $L_1$ | System relevance | A difference systematically changes the current system's state, capabilities, relations, resources, reachable domain, maintenance, or recovery conditions | “Beneficial / harmful,” worth maintaining, or that options should be expanded |
| $L_2$ | Regulatory polarity | The system's own organization makes some relevant differences enter directional regulation such as approach, avoidance, maintenance, recovery, suppression, or search | Subjective experience, normative ranking, or all sources of orientation |
| gate | Experience-status gate | Only an object already supported by proportionate reasons for treatment as an experiencing subject enters experiential-layer analysis | Subjectivity proved by self-maintenance, reward, goal representation, or complex behavior |
| $L_{3a}$ | Experiential polarity | A state becomes part of the subject's own experience in a non-neutral way—positive, negative, attractive, aversive, comfortable, painful, etc. | A specific action duty, or a one-to-one mapping between behavior and experience |
| $L_{3b}$ | First-order orientation | Directional structures actually entering current distinction, judgment, or action, such as approach, avoidance, maintenance, acquisition, stopping, protection, or commitment | Reflective endorsement by the subject, or stable value |
| $L_4$ | Reflective endorsement | The subject makes existing orientations, experiences, sources, consequences, and conflicts objects of judgment, and endorses, revises, holds, or rejects them | A true self, ultimate value, or immunity from further revision |

The purpose of this ladder is not to compress value into a single linear mechanism, but to prevent responsibility shortcuts.

### 1. $L_0$: consequence difference

Under an explicit object, time scale, and real-world conditions, if:

$$
x,y\rightarrow R_{t+1}^{(x)},R_{t+1}^{(y)},
\qquad
R_{t+1}^{(x)}\neq R_{t+1}^{(y)},
$$

this establishes only a current consequence difference. It does not prove which side is better and does not prove that the system has formed a directional organization around the difference.

Judgments of consequences remain constrained by boundary, scale, material acquisition, and mode of distinction. Local outcomes must not be silently expanded into whole-system outcomes.

### 2. $L_1$: system relevance

System relevance must carry at least:

$$
(\text{system boundary},\text{scale},\text{time},\text{variables},\text{conditions}).
$$

The same change can improve local output while weakening long-term recovery, or expand one subject's reachable domain while transferring costs to others. Therefore “affects persistence,” “expands capability,” and “preserves more options” remain factual structures and do not automatically acquire value status.

### 3. $L_2$: regulatory polarity

Only when a difference actually enters perception, comparison, feedback, control, judgment, or action organization such that later system paths form a relatively stable but revisable directional difference do we posit regulatory polarity.

Working notation:

$$
x\succ_{\mathrm{reg}}y
$$

means only a relative tendency in the current regulatory structure, not normative “betterness.”

Regulatory polarity can be one source of `G`, but:

$$
\text{regulatory polarity}\neq G.
$$

Orientation may also arise from inheritance, teaching, social relations, institutional requirements, strategic interaction, counterfactuals, long-term commitments, and other structures. This document does not reduce all value to survival, reward, or a single objective function.

## III. Experience-Status Gate

The **experience-status gate** states that analysis of experiential polarity, subject-relative self-involvement, and first-person value formation is permitted only when the current object, under relevant domain evidence and the working boundary of subjectivity in the [Theory of Cognitive Responsibility](theory-of-cognitive-responsibility.md), already has reason to be treated as a subject with its own experience.

Thus:

$$
\boxed{
\text{functional self-maintenance}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{first-person experience}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{functional agency}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{subjectivity}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{regulatory polarity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{experiential polarity}
}
$$

Reward signals, loss functions, target variables, self-models, linguistic self-report, complex behavior, persistent memory, or approach/avoid behavior cannot individually serve here as sufficient evidence of subjective experience; absence of any one of them cannot individually disprove experience either.

## IV. Experience, First-Order Orientation, and Reflection

After the experience-status gate is passed, $V_i^{\mathrm{exp}}(x)$ denotes subject $i$'s current position of experiential polarity toward state $x$. It does not commit to a common numerical dimension and is not a utility function.

Experience cannot be directly identified with behavior or first-order desire:

$$
V_i^{\mathrm{exp}}(x)\not\Leftrightarrow A_i(x),
$$

$$
V_i^{\mathrm{exp}}(x)\not\Rightarrow G_i^{(0)}(x),
\qquad
G_i^{(0)}(x)\not\Rightarrow V_i^{\mathrm{exp}}(x).
$$

First-order orientations may arise from bodily regulation, experience, learning, habit, teaching, relations, institutions, strategic interaction, external tasks, and other sources. They may be affected by impulse, fear, social reward and punishment, manipulation, or incorrect models, and are therefore not identical with the subject's value commitments.

Reflective endorsement makes existing orientations, experiences, sources, consequences, and conflicts into objects of judgment again. Working representation:

$$
G_i^{(0)}
\xrightarrow{\Phi,\ J_{\mathrm{meta}},\ \Gamma}
G_i^{(1)}.
$$

Here $G_i^{(1)}$ denotes only the current state of endorsement, revision, or rejection after reflection. It does not denote a true self, ultimate value, or final correctness.

## V. Individual Value Commitment

An **individual value commitment** is a current endorsement by a subject, under the reality, experience, desires, relations, consequences, unknowns, and reflective conditions currently available, of some orientations that can enter later judgment and action, with a willingness to bear their real costs, maintenance conditions, and revisability within a proportionate scope.

Working representation:

$$
\{G_i^{(1)},S_i,R_i,V_i^{\mathrm{exp}},U_i,\mathcal H_i,\ldots\}
\rightsquigarrow
C_i^{\mathrm{value}}.
$$

$C_i^{\mathrm{value}}$ is not a complete preference function and is not normative closure.

Commitment is not determined by a single measure of desire strength, emotional intensity, sacrifice, or duration. Revisability does not mean absence of commitment; commitment does not mean refusal of revision.

## VI. Tracing Value Sources

Value-source tracing is used to acquire material about how current value commitments formed, not to find one “true cause.” Candidate sources may include bodily and experiential history, care and relations, inherited or preconfigured structure, structured teaching, group and institutional incentives, strategic interaction, habit and path dependence, trauma and loss, and later active endorsement, rejection, and reorganization by the subject.

Sources may be multiple, interacting, and changed by later reflection.

Maintain:

$$
\text{being able to explain a source}\not\Rightarrow\text{being able to cancel the value},
$$

$$
\text{a natural source}\not\Rightarrow\text{value legitimacy},
$$

$$
\text{a socialized source}\not\Rightarrow\text{false value}.
$$

## VII. Reopening Under Value Conflict

**Reopening under value conflict** means that when the current value structure persistently generates conflicts significant for judgment, value, facts, subject boundary, material acquisition, organizational structure, or problem definition are reopened rather than suppressing the conflict with existing commitments.

Typical triggers include:

- long-term conflict between experiential polarity and first-order desire;
- persistent conflict between first-order desire and reflective endorsement;
- multiple endorsed orientations that cannot be jointly maintained;
- refutation of key facts on which the current value depends;
- value realization that persistently creates unexpected loss or transfers major costs to others;
- new relations, bodily states, or real-world conditions that change experiential structure;
- discovery of coercion, manipulation, inability to exit, or structural dependency;
- significant change in the value structure after external rewards and punishments are removed.

As working notation:

$$
T_G
\rightsquigarrow
\Gamma_G
\rightsquigarrow
\operatorname{reopen}(G,D,F,S,\mathcal Q,V,\text{problem}).
$$

Reopening requires renewed judgment only. It does not automatically require changing values or changing real-world conditions.

## VIII. Boundary From Individual Value to Intersubjective Norms

Even after stable individual value commitments form, maintain:

$$
\boxed{
C_i^{\mathrm{value}}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{others ought to comply}
}
$$

Subjective experience, pain, fear, satisfaction, attachment, or damage to dignity may be important factual material in normative argument, but:

$$
\text{normatively relevant fact}
\neq
\text{normative conclusion}.
$$

When value enters shared affairs, affected parties, loss, exit, power asymmetry, non-compensable boundaries, decision and stopping authority, and institutional legitimacy must be handled separately. These questions are handed to the shared-affairs and normative boundaries of the [Theory of Determinative Responsibility](theory-of-determinative-responsibility.md), [Action Responsibility Practice](action-responsibility-practice.md), and the relevant legal and normative procedures.

## IX. Open, Closure, and Domain Handoff

Value questions may be provisionally closed for expression, comparison, planning, or action, but closure does not mean that the subject knows all of its real desires, that current values will not change, that value sources have been completely explained, or that current commitments have acquired authority over others.

On entering a concrete domain, one must further obtain:

1. whether there is proportionate reason to treat the current object as an experiencing subject;
2. how experiential, behavioral, bodily, and environmental materials are acquired and cross-validated;
3. how affect, motivation, habit, impulse, preference, and commitment are distinguished;
4. what mechanisms support reflection, inhibition, reappraisal, and long-term value change;
5. what cultural, institutional, relational, and power structures enter value formation;
6. which intersubjective conflicts involve rights, authorization, and non-compensable loss;
7. what facts would force the current explanation of value sources to be narrowed, reorganized, or abandoned.

These concrete mechanisms and evidence are not presupposed by this document.

## X. Central Proposition

The entire chain of value genesis must preserve:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{consequences differ} &\not\Rightarrow \text{system relevance};\\
\text{system relevance} &\not\Rightarrow \text{regulatory polarity};\\
\text{regulatory polarity} &\not\Rightarrow \text{subjective experience};\\
\text{experiential polarity} &\not\Rightarrow \text{first-order orientation in the same direction};\\
\text{first-order orientation} &\not\Rightarrow \text{reflective endorsement by the subject};\\
\text{reflective endorsement} &\not\Rightarrow \text{final correctness of value};\\
\text{subject's value commitment} &\not\Rightarrow \text{legitimate constraint on others}.
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The central proposition is: real differences can become endogenous sources of orientation in finite systems, but stronger value claims become qualified only after responsibility for subject-experience status, reflective endorsement, normative handoff, and other distinct responsibilities has been separately carried.
